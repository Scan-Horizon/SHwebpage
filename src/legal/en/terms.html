11.5. Moderation: policies, procedures, measures and tools

This article is published pursuant to Article 14 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065. It applies to usernames, to structured clan feed messages and to clan chat messages.

11.5.1. Four levels. Moderation relies on automated filtering (11.5.2), on reporting and blocking by members (Articles 11.4 and 11.9), on volunteer player moderators (11.5.3) and on the Publisher's team, which alone decides on bans, handles complaints and supervises moderators.

11.5.2. Automated tools. Usernames are subject, upon creation, to automated filtering against a list of prohibited terms. This filtering is applied without human intervention and may result in the immediate rejection of a username.

Chat messages are filtered by the server before they are posted, after a normalization designed to defeat simple workarounds (accents, digits substituted for letters, repeated letters). A message containing a term from the most serious list (hate, threats, serious insults) is rejected, with an explanation shown to the User. A term from the list of common profanities is replaced with asterisks before posting.

Automatic limits also apply: at most one message per second and twenty per minute; rejection of the same message sent three times in a row. A message of more than three words written more than 70% in capital letters is not rejected: it is posted in lower case.

Enhanced protections for Users aged 13 to 15. For them, the list of common profanities is treated like the most serious list: the message is rejected instead of being posted with asterisks. Their sending limits are reduced to one message every two seconds and ten per minute.

These tools do not use any artificial intelligence system. They act only on the message at the time it is sent, or through the provisional hiding described in Article 11.4, and do not by themselves impose any mute, ban or suspension. Separate automated measures, intended for service security and to fight scan automation, are described in Article 8 bis of the Privacy Policy; they do not concern content and are governed by a separate regime.

11.5.3. Volunteer player moderators. The Publisher may entrust volunteer Users aged at least eighteen with a chat moderator role, exercised only in clans other than their own. This role is granted and withdrawn at the Publisher's discretion, without notice.

This role is voluntary and gives rise to no consideration of any kind: no money, no currency or benefit usable in the Application, no premium access. A visible distinguishing sign (badge) may however be granted. No obligation is imposed on the moderator: no volume of reports, no hours, no deadline. They may stop at any time, without giving a reason, by no longer using the moderation tools or by informing the Publisher. Withdrawal of the role is the only consequence that may be attached to its exercise.

Before accessing the moderation tools, the moderator accepts the Moderators' Charter: confidentiality, impartiality, no screenshots or sharing, no contact outside the Application with the players concerned.

The moderator may hide or delete a message, issue a warning, mute a User for up to seven days, and dismiss a report. Where the measure they consider necessary exceeds their powers (in particular a ban), they may propose it to the Publisher's team, which alone decides; such a proposal applies no measure. To handle a report, they see the reported message and the surrounding messages, with their authors' usernames and avatars, as well as the reporting party's username and any comment they added. To assess the situation, they may also view the moderation measures already taken against the reported player, as well as the messages that player posted over the last thirty days in clans other than the moderator's own; each viewing of these messages is logged. They never see the email address or other account data. Each of their actions is logged and may be reversed by the Publisher's team, which reviews a sample of actions every week. This review concerns the soundness of decisions, not the moderators' activity. The Publisher remains responsible for all moderation measures, including those taken by a player moderator.

11.5.4. Human review. Any restriction decision other than the automatic rejection of a username or a message and the provisional hiding under Article 11.4 (suspension, account deletion, withdrawal of an already-assigned username, hiding or deletion of a message, warning, mute, chat ban, exclusion from a leaderboard or challenge) is taken after human review, by a player moderator or by the Publisher's team.

11.5.5. Measures that may be taken. Rejection or withdrawal of a username and a request to choose a new one; hiding or deletion of a message; warning, which restricts no feature but remains in the player's moderation history for twelve months and may be taken into account in the event of a repeat offence; mute, which prevents writing in the chat of any clan until it expires, including after changing clan; chat ban, decided by the Publisher's team alone; exclusion from a clan, a challenge or a leaderboard; cancellation of improperly obtained rewards; temporary suspension; termination of the account.

As a guide only, without binding the assessment of each situation:

  • spam or repeated messages: 1-hour mute, then 24 hours for a repeat offence;
  • isolated insult: warning or 24-hour mute, then 7 days;
  • harassment: 7-day mute, then chat ban;
  • hate, threats, sexual content: chat ban from the first occurrence.
